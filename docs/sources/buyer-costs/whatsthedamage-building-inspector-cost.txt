What Actually Moves the Building Inspector Cost in Australia
Property size and type. A two-bedroom unit takes an hour; a large house with subfloor and roof void takes three.
Age and construction. Period homes, weatherboard on stumps and heritage stock cost more to inspect properly.
Combined vs separate. Building and pest booked together is cheaper than two separate visits.
Access. Locked subfloors, steep roofs and tenanted properties add time or a second visit.
Turnaround. Same-day reports before an auction sometimes carry a premium.
Do you actually need one?
Yes for almost any established house, anything with timber framing or flooring, and any property bought at auction where there's no cooling-off period.
A brand-new build still under statutory warranty, or a recently inspected strata unit where you can read the existing report, may not need a full combined inspection.
The honest test: the inspection costs a fraction of one percent of the purchase price. If a $20,000 defect would change your offer, it's worth it.
Red flags when comparing building inspectors
Referred by the selling agent. Always engage your own inspector.
No licence or registration where your state requires one, or no professional indemnity insurance.
No sample report available before you book.
Report turnaround longer than 48 hours when you have a cooling-off deadline.
Quotes well under the local range, which usually means a drive-by visual with no roof void or subfloor.
How to Keep the Building Inspector Cost Sane in Australia
1
Book building and pest together: The bundle discount is real.
2
Skip add-ons unless there's a reason: thermal imaging and drone roof checks are for specific suspicions, not every house.
3
Ask about pre-auction pricing: If you're outbid you still pay, so some inspectors discount repeat bookings.
4
Compare on scope, not price: A cheap report that misses the subfloor is the expensive option.
5
Use the report: Most findings are negotiation leverage on price or repairs before settlement.
Frequently Asked Questions — Building Inspector Cost in Australia
How much is a building and pest inspection in Australia?
A combined building and pest inspection on a standard house is typically $450–$900, rising to $700–$1,400 for large, heritage or hard-to-access homes. Building-only on a unit runs $300–$600.
What does a building inspection include?
A visual inspection of accessible areas — structure, roof, subfloor, walls, wet areas, drainage — reported to Australian Standard AS 4349.1. It does not include invasive testing.
What's the difference between a building and a pest inspection?
The building inspection covers structural and general defects. The pest inspection covers termite and timber-pest activity and damage. Most buyers combine them.
Can I get a refund if I'm outbid at auction?
No. The inspection is paid regardless of whether you buy. Some inspectors offer a discount on subsequent bookings.
How long does the report take?
Most inspectors deliver a photo report within 24 hours, many the same day.
Should I use the inspector the agent recommends?
No. The selling agent works for the vendor. Engage your own inspector independently.
