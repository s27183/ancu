Collaborative Australian Protected Areas Database 2024
Last updated: 13 April 2026
During 2024, we compiled information on terrestrial and marine protected areas from:
Australian Government agencies
state and territory nature conservation agencies
Indigenous Protected Area (IPA) managers
private protected area managers.
The Collaborative Australian Protected Areas Database (CAPAD) 2024 is the result.
CAPAD 2024 shows that on 30 June 2024, Australia had:
14,575 terrestrial protected areas, covering 22.57% of Australia’s landmass
319 marine protected areas and 11 Sea Country Indigenous Protected Areas that cover 48.9% of Australian waters.
Since then, 10 Indigenous Protected Areas have been dedicated. These add more than 17.7 million additional hectares to Australia’s terrestrial protected areas. As of September 2025, terrestrial protected areas cover 24.92% of our landmass.
Australia’s marine protected area estate also grew in this time and now covers 52% of Australian waters.
CAPAD 2024 includes:
Information on interim protected areas. These are land parcels identified to become protected areas. The process for formal recognition is underway.
Sea Country IPAs. These areas were included in CAPAD for the first time in 2024. This recognises and respects First Nations peoples’ management of Sea Country. Their inclusion contributes to Australia’s domestic and international reporting on marine protection. However, because they are not created through a parliamentary process, they are not part of the National Representative System of Marine Protected Areas (NRSMPA).
The Australian Government is continuing to work with data suppliers to improve data submitted to CAPAD. Find out more about the geographic and descriptive information provided in the technical specifications.

CAPAD 2024 Spatial Data
GIS data for CAPAD 2024 is available on the Find Environmental Data (FED) website .
Access to this data is by the Creative Commons Attribution (CC-BY) licence model. The information is available in ArcGIS format.
The CAPAD 2024 dataset integrates terrestrial and marine spatial data into a single spatial layer for the first time. Area statistic reporting for the National Reserve System (NRS) and the NRSMPA remain separate.
The combined data means that users can display terrestrial or marine areas individually or together.
