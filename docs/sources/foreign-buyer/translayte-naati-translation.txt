What Type of Certified Translation is Approved in Australia?
If you are submitting non-English documents as part of an official application in Australia, then you may need a NAATI translation.
Certified translation requirements in Australia
Translations for official use must be certified by an accredited translator in Australia. A NAATI translation can include:
Translator's full name
NAATI practitioner or credential number
NAATI credential details
Language and language direction
Translation date
Translator's signature
NAATI translator stamp
Certification details confirming the translator's work
Many official institutions, government departments, and legal bodies require certified translations to ensure accuracy, reliability, and adherence to specific standards. These translations are essential for applications such as immigration, academic admissions, and legal processes. Using a certified translation service, such as one provided by a certified NAATI translator, helps ensure your documents meet requirements and are accepted without issues.
What is a NAATI Translation?
A NAATI translation is a translation completed or certified by a translator who holds an appropriate NAATI credential.
NAATI, the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters, sets Australia's national standards and certifies translators and interpreters. Its current translator credentials include Certified Advanced Translator, Certified Translator, and Recognised Practising Translator.
For applications where Home Affairs requires an Australian translation, the translator's NAATI practitioner number is an important part of the submission. Home Affairs specifically instructs applicants using an Australian translator to include the translator's NAATI practitioner number.
Why is NAATI Translation Important?
Official Recognition : Australian government agencies require and recognise translations by NAATI-certified translators for legal, immigration, and formal purposes.
Accuracy and Reliability: Using a NAATI-credentialed translator ensures accurate, reliable translations and reduces the risk of errors in official documents.
Legal Compliance : Translations by NAATI-certified translators meet legal requirements and help avoid delays in legal and government applications.
Immigration Applications: A NAATI certification is mandatory for translations related to visa, residency, and citizenship applications.
Professional Standards : NAATI ensures that translators listed on the NAATI certified translator list meet strict professional standards, protecting the integrity of your documents and ensuring acceptance every time.
When Will You Need NAATI Translations?
A certified translation done by a NAATI-certified translator is often required to meet Australian requirements. Some common examples include:
Immigration applications : When applying for a visa or permanent residency, you must provide NAATI document translations of key documents, such as birth certificates, marriage certificates, and police clearance certificates.
Academic applications : When applying to an Australian university or college, you may need to submit certified translations of your transcripts, diplomas, and other relevant academic documents.
Legal matters : In court proceedings and other legal matters, certified translations of documents like contracts, affidavits, and witness statements are often required to ensure their validity and credibility.
Employment: If you’re seeking employment in Australia, employers may request translations of your employment history, references, or qualifications from a NAATI-Accredited Translator to confirm accuracy.
Medical and insurance purposes : If you need to provide medical records or insurance documents, certified translations may be necessary to ensure the information is accurate and easily understood by all parties involved.

Translations Produced Abroad, or for Australian Embassies
A translator based outside Australia does not need to be certified by NAATI. But they must endorse the translation with their full name, address, telephone number, and details of their qualifications and experience in the language being translated. This process helps speed up applications, as it can be difficult to access a NAATI translator overseas.
When submitting an English translation, a copy of the original document must also be included. Failure to provide the source document may delay processing and result in the translation not being accepted as part of your visa application.

Who is a NAATI-Certified Translator?
A NAATI-certified translator is a professional translator certified by the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) in Australia. NAATI is Australia's national standards and certification body for translators and interpreters.
Can NAATI Certify Documents?
No, NAATI does not certify documents. They certify translators, who then certify translated documents with their stamp/signature.
Do Translators Have to be NAATI Certified?
In Australia, translators don't need to be NAATI certified to work as translators. However, NAATI certification is required if you want to work as a certified translator in Australia, as NAATI is the only body that certifies translators. Translators without NAATI certification are independent professional translators, not certified translators.
What is the Difference Between a Translation done by a NAATI Translator & a Regular Translation?
A NAATI-certified translation is performed by a translator accredited by the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) in Australia, ensuring it meets the official standards required by government agencies, courts, and other institutions. This certification is crucial for legal, immigration, and other formal uses. In contrast, a regular translation may be accurate but lacks this certification and is generally not accepted for official purposes, so it suits only informal or general content.
